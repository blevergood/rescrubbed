# Design narrative: how this application came to look the way it does

_Generated by the Niobium FHE Application Design AI Assistant (FHEanna)._

A colleague reading this should finish knowing what was built, why, and which
alternatives were rejected.

---

## The problem

Score a patient's risk of returning to hospital within 30 days of discharge,
using a model that runs on infrastructure the hospital will not trust with the
patient's record.

## The shape of the answer

A vendor sells readmission scoring as a service. The hospital encrypts a patient
record and sends it; the vendor computes a risk band on the encrypted data
without being able to read it; the hospital decrypts the band. The hospital keeps
its records, the vendor keeps its model.

---

## Stage 1: the privacy model

Mapping the parties was straightforward. The hospital holds records and the only
decryption key; the vendor holds the model and never gets a key; the cloud
operator sees ciphertext.

The fourth question in the privacy model asks what the *answer* gives away.

The model is a weighted tally over 28 fields. If the service returns the exact
score, a hospital can submit 29 chosen records, collect 29 scores, and solve a
linear system for every weight. Returning a probability instead is an invertible
relabelling of the same number.

**Decision: the service returns a risk band, low, moderate or high, computed
inside the encrypted computation.** Each answer carries roughly 1.6 bits, and the
published estimate for extracting the model from band answers alone runs to
10²–10⁴ queries.

Two supporting points:

- LACE and HOSPITAL, the two established bedside scores, both band. The care
  decision they feed is binary: enrol in transitional care, or do not.
- Banding does not close the extraction channel, only narrows it. The threat
  model records the residual attack and the query rate limiting the deployment
  needs.

### Where each step runs

The rule: nothing sensitive runs in readable form on the vendor's machine, and
the encrypted part stays small, because encrypted work is expensive.

Feature extraction, meaning diagnosis-code grouping and one-hot encoding, is
hostile to encrypted computation: table lookups and string matching. It is also
sensitive. The hospital already holds the record in readable form, so the work
runs client-side in the clear and never needs encrypting. Only the weighted tally
and the band decision run on the vendor's side, because only the vendor has the
weights.

### Scaling folded into the weights

The obvious design has the hospital standardise each field using statistics from
the vendor's training data, which means shipping those statistics to every
customer. Instead the vendor folds that scaling into its own weights. The
hospital clips each field to a published clinical range and maps it to 0–1.
Nothing derived from the training data crosses the boundary.

Stage 6 describes what else that buys.

---

## Stage 2: feasibility

Four tests. The computation is a fixed sequence with no branching, which
encrypted computation requires since it cannot branch on values it cannot see. It
is pure arithmetic on real numbers with no bit-level operations. Its chain of
dependent multiplications is 10 links against a practical ceiling of 25–30, so it
needs no refresh machinery. The nightly batch mode scores 32,768 patients in a
single pass.

The single-patient mode uses one slot in 32,768. That is the price of interactive
latency rather than throughput.

---

## Stage 3: the reference model

**Data.** The UCI *Diabetes 130-US Hospitals* set: 101,766 real de-identified
inpatient encounters from 130 US hospitals, the standard public benchmark for
this task. A synthetic dataset behind an invented model form would be a toy, and
this set being public raises no patient privacy issue during development.

**Cohort.** Two filters:

- Dropped encounters ending in death or hospice transfer (2,423). A patient who
  died cannot be readmitted, so keeping them teaches the model that "will not
  return" is predictable from the discharge disposition.
- Kept every remaining encounter, including repeat admissions. The deployed
  system scores discharges rather than patients, so a frequent admitter carries
  the weight of each of their discharges, and those repeat visits are where much
  of the signal sits. Discarding them costs 0.015 AUC.

The train and test halves are split on patient_nbr, so no individual appears on
both sides. That, rather than throwing away repeat visits, is what stops the
model memorising a person.

99,343 encounters across 69,990 patients remain, 11.6% readmitted within 30 days.

**Model form.** Regularised logistic regression. On this dataset published
gradient boosting reaches around 0.667 ranking quality and published logistic
regression around 0.642; a gradient-boosting model fitted on our feature set
reaches 0.659 against our linear model's 0.651. A tree ensemble is all
comparisons and branching, which is hostile to encrypted computation.

Its second property is validation: scikit-learn's implementation is a
permissively-licensed external oracle, so the twin is checked against something
we did not write.

**Features.** 28, spanning the constructs the established scores use: length of
stay, admission acuity, comorbidity burden, prior utilisation, plus routine
record fields. The set is enumerated in the README; columns of the source dataset
not listed there are not used. Extreme values are clipped rather than rejected;
Stage 6 covers what that buys.

**Metric.** Ranking quality (AUC) and precision/recall on the high-risk band. At
an 11.6% readmission rate, a model that predicts "nobody returns" scores 91%
accuracy at zero recall.

## Stage 4: scheme

CKKS. The computation is real-valued arithmetic, which is what CKKS is built for,
and it is what the target hardware runs.

---

## Stage 5: the circuit

```
score = sum_j (weight_j x field_j) + bias      1 level
band  = P(score)                               9 levels
```

Three choices worth recording:

**One polynomial over the whole staircase.** The obvious implementation compares
the score against the moderate threshold, again against the high threshold, then
adds the two answers. A single polynomial approximates the two-step staircase
directly, at the same depth, with half the multiplications and one output
ciphertext.

**Column-major packing for the batch.** One ciphertext per field, one slot per
patient. The linear layer becomes ciphertext × plaintext multiply-accumulate
across ciphertexts, needing no rotation keys, which are normally the dominant key
cost. The row-major alternative needs 32 ciphertexts instead of 28, plus rotation
keys, for the same result.

**A second layout for the single-patient path.** Reusing the batch layout to
score one patient uploads 364 MiB. Instead that patient's 28 fields go into one
ciphertext and are summed across slots by rotate-and-add: 13 MiB per query, at
the cost of 5 rotation keys and a second code path.

## Stage 6: parameters

### The provable range

Every field is clipped into 0–1 before encryption and the weights are fixed, so
the score is confined to **[-4.18, +1.65]** by arithmetic over the input contract
rather than by estimate from a sample.

The polynomial approximating the band staircase is valid only over the interval
it was fitted to. Outside it the polynomial diverges and the result fails to
decrypt. The common alternative is to estimate that interval from observed data,
publish an input filter, reject the patients that escape it, and then show the
rejected set does not over-represent the sick patients. Here nothing is rejected,
and the batch mode, where one bad record would spoil all 32,768, is safe by
construction.

Clipping is what buys this. It is legitimate here because every clipped field is
a continuous clinical count where saturating at the bound preserves the ordering
the model uses. Categorical codes would be wrong to clip; none are.

### The sweep

42 combinations of transition steepness and polynomial degree, measured against
the reference on held-out patients, all on the twin, which runs in seconds rather
than on encrypted runs that take minutes.

Too gentle a transition and the band boundaries blur: steepness 8 misbands 354
patients regardless of degree. Too steep and the polynomial cannot follow it at
affordable degree: steepness 48 at degree 31 misbands 1,973. The optimum sits at
steepness 24.

| Option | Depth | Patients misbanded (of 99,343) | Batch upload |
|---|---|---|---|
| degree 127 | 11 | 8 | 336 MiB |
| **degree 191** | **12** | **1** | **364 MiB** |

**Chosen: degree 191.** Expressed as percentages the choice reads as a tradeoff
("8% more data, 10% more time"). In absolute terms it is 28 MiB per batch of
32,768 patients and one extra multiplication step, for agreement with the
reference model on every patient but one.

### Other settings

Ring dimension 2^16, mandatory for the target hardware and independently required
for 128-bit security at this modulus size; 45 bits per level; 60-bit first
modulus; automatic rescaling.

Depth is set two levels above what the circuit nominally consumes. The naive
per-operation count can undercount real consumption under automatic rescaling, so
a nominal spare of one level can be an effective spare of zero. The resulting
decode budget has 94 bits of headroom.

## Stage 7: the gate

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Weighted tally vs scikit-learn's own implementation | identical (0.00e+00) |
| Decision rule matches the reference | two threshold comparisons |
| Band agreement, 25,010 held-out patients | **100.00%** (0 differ) |
| High-band precision | 0.2118 → 0.2118 (delta +0.00000) |
| High-band recall | 0.2801 → 0.2801 (delta +0.00000) |
| Worst polynomial error across the whole provable range | 3.04e-04 |
| Decode budget | +94 bits spare |
| Rotate-and-sum path reproduces the batch tally | 4.44e-16 |

Recorded noise tolerance: at the expected encryption noise of 1e-5, zero of
25,010 patients could change band. The Stage 8 pass criterion is therefore
measured error below 1e-4 with 100% band agreement.

## Roads not taken

- **Gradient boosting or a neural network.** Buys about 0.01 ranking quality
  (0.659 against 0.651) for a circuit of comparisons and branching.
- **Returning the exact score or probability.** Hands the vendor's model to the
  hospital in 29 queries.
- **Two separate threshold comparisons.** One polynomial over the whole staircase
  costs the same depth and half the multiplications.
- **Row-major packing for the batch.** More ciphertexts and rotation keys, for
  the same answer. Adopted for the single-patient path, where it saves 28× on
  upload.
- **An input filter that rejects out-of-range patients.** Clipping makes the
  range guarantee provable, so nothing is rejected and the composition question
  does not arise.
- **Bootstrapping**, the refresh machinery for deep circuits. At depth 12 against
  a ceiling of 25–30 it is not needed. It would cost 10–15 levels of overhead and
  commit the deployment to a software-only path.

## Implementation notes (Stage 8)

The encrypted build required no change to the design. Every parameter chosen in
Stages 5–6 held, the predicted decode margin of +94 bits was confirmed by four
clean runs, and the predicted boundary sizes matched to within a megabyte.

Four notes for anyone modifying this:

**No slot masking is needed before the polynomial.** The usual trap in a
rotate-and-sum design is that the slots other than slot 0 hold partial sums,
which can fall outside the polynomial's fitted interval and cause the whole
result to fail decryption. A partial sum here ranges over a subset of the same
weighted terms as the full sum, so it is bounded by the full sum's range and
lands inside the same interval. Confirming that saved a multiplication step in
the single-patient path. The argument is written out in `app/rescrubbed.hpp`.

**Both request shapes land at the same depth**, so one set of parameters and one
context serve both.

**Two build frictions**, recorded so they are not rediscovered: OpenFHE's
ciphertext × plaintext overloads bind a mutable `Plaintext&`, so the plaintext
operands cannot be passed as `const`; and Python's `argparse` reads a bare
`--cpu` as a flag rather than a value, so the demo passes `--flag=--cpu`.

**The measured cost matched the design-time estimate closely.** The user's
challenge during Stage 6, that percentages are meaningless without absolutes,
changed how the choice was framed: stated in real units, the "10% more expensive"
option cost 28 MiB per 32,768 patients.

**The Fog run produced identical answers**, confirming the deployment path end to
end. Most of its elapsed time is the transfer of a 614 MB recording of the
computation to the worker rather than arithmetic, which is visible in the way the
figure varies between otherwise identical runs.
